# LinkedIn Profile Analysis Rubric

Use this rubric for full audits, focused scorecards, section reviews, and target-audience fit analysis. Score each dimension from 1 to 5 only when the supplied profile material provides enough evidence.

## Scoring Scale

- **5**: Strong, specific, and immediately useful for the stated goal.
- **4**: Good foundation with minor clarity, evidence, or targeting gaps.
- **3**: Understandable but generic, incomplete, or under-evidenced.
- **2**: Unclear, inconsistent, or missing important trust signals.
- **1**: Actively weak for the stated goal or likely to create confusion.
- **Unknown**: Not enough evidence in the supplied material.

## Dimensions

### Positioning Clarity

Evaluate whether the first-screen impression explains who the person is, what category they operate in, and why the intended audience should care.

Look for:
- Headline names a role, category, audience, problem, or outcome.
- About section reinforces the same professional promise.
- Current role and experience make the positioning believable.
- The profile avoids empty status labels such as "visionary", "results-driven", or "growth-focused" unless backed by specifics.

Common fixes:
- Rewrite the headline around role + category + proof or role + audience + outcome.
- Replace broad claims with a sharper professional category.
- Move the strongest proof point into the opening lines.

### Credibility Density

Evaluate whether the profile backs claims with observable evidence.

Look for:
- Metrics, named products, markets, team sizes, budgets, revenue scope, publications, talks, open-source work, patents, awards, or recognizable employers.
- Clear before/after outcomes.
- Featured assets that prove expertise.
- Recommendations or endorsements that match the intended positioning.

Common fixes:
- Add quantified scope where truthful.
- Convert responsibilities into outcomes.
- Add 2-4 featured assets that support the profile narrative.
- Use placeholders for unverified claims, such as `[metric]`, until the user confirms them.

### Audience Fit

Evaluate whether the profile is written for the intended reader.

For recruiters:
- Role titles, seniority, skills, industry keywords, location/remote preferences, and measurable accomplishments should be easy to scan.

For buyers or partners:
- Problems solved, customer category, proof, offer, and contact path should be clear.

For investors:
- Market category, founder credibility, traction, strategic insight, and company narrative should be visible.

For creators:
- Topic promise, expertise lane, posting consistency, and audience benefit should be clear.

Common fixes:
- Replace inward-facing biography with reader-facing relevance.
- Add language the target audience already uses.
- Remove details that dilute the primary audience.

### Search Discoverability

Evaluate whether the profile naturally contains the terms someone would search.

Look for:
- Target role titles and variants.
- Domain and industry keywords.
- Tools, methods, certifications, and technical skills.
- Seniority indicators.
- Geographic or remote-work signals when relevant.

Common fixes:
- Add keyword-rich but natural headline and About phrasing.
- Ensure experience entries include specific technologies, markets, and functions.
- Align skills with the user's target role or audience.

### Narrative Coherence

Evaluate whether all profile sections reinforce one professional story.

Look for:
- Headline, About, current role, featured items, experience, and activity point to the same market/category.
- Career transitions are explained rather than left ambiguous.
- The profile has a clear through-line instead of a list of disconnected achievements.

Common fixes:
- Add a short transition sentence in About.
- Reorder proof points by relevance to the target goal.
- Remove or de-emphasize outdated positioning.

### Conversion Readiness

Evaluate whether the profile creates an obvious next step.

Look for:
- Contact path, portfolio, company site, calendar link, newsletter, GitHub, personal site, or media kit where appropriate.
- Featured section includes assets that reduce friction.
- About section ends with a relevant invitation or next action.

Common fixes:
- Add a precise call to action.
- Feature the strongest external proof.
- Clarify what kinds of opportunities or conversations are welcome.

### Content Authority

Evaluate profile activity only when content data is provided.

Look for:
- Consistent themes tied to the desired positioning.
- Posts that demonstrate judgment, not only announcements.
- Comments that add expertise and visibility in the right network.
- Engagement with relevant people, companies, and topics.

Common fixes:
- Define 3-5 recurring content pillars.
- Turn experience into teachable insights.
- Pin or feature the strongest proof content.

## Prioritization Heuristic

Rank recommendations by:

1. First-screen clarity: headline, banner, opening About lines, current role.
2. Trust proof: metrics, featured assets, experience outcomes.
3. Audience alignment: keywords, language, proof type, call to action.
4. Completeness: skills, recommendations, media, contact path.
5. Content strategy: posts, comments, themes, cadence.

High-impact quick wins usually include headline rewrite, About opening rewrite, experience bullet tightening, Featured section cleanup, and skills alignment.
