# Parameter Research Context

Last reviewed: 2026-05-27.

Use this file when you need more evaluation context for the scorecard dimensions and section metrics. It translates current research and stable LinkedIn product documentation into practical audit questions.

## Recency Rule

Prefer research published or refreshed in the last 90 days when explaining why a score matters. As of 2026-05-27, the 90-day window starts on 2026-02-26.

If no suitable source exists inside the 90-day window for a parameter, use the latest official platform documentation or the most recent credible category research and label the source as stable context, not fresh research. Do not pretend older sources are recent.

## Source Tiers

Use sources in this order:

1. Current LinkedIn product, Talent Solutions, Sales Navigator, Economic Graph, or Marketing Solutions material.
2. Recent reputable research from firms such as Gartner, Edelman, SurveyMonkey, G2, Reuters, or peer-reviewed/preprint research when it directly relates to recruiting, professional search, B2B buying, trust, or AI-mediated discovery.
3. Stable LinkedIn Help documentation for product mechanics such as profile sections, Featured, Skills, Recruiter filters, and Talent Insights.
4. Practitioner content only as weak context. Do not use social posts or Reddit as primary evidence for scoring.

## Dimension Context

### Screenshot Coverage

Why it matters:

- A profile audit is only as reliable as the visible evidence. Recent people-search research emphasizes explicit criteria, qualified result coverage, and information utility; the same principle applies here.
- LinkedIn Economic Graph methodology also depends on members updating profile fields, which means screenshots can show useful signals but may miss hidden, stale, or private details.

Evaluate:

- Are the core sections visible: Intro/Hero, About, Featured, Activity, Experience, Skills, Recommendations, and Education/certifications?
- Is text readable enough to support copy-level feedback?
- Are expandable sections open when the user asks for rewrites?
- Are recommendations, Featured assets, or posts visible when trust/content scoring matters?

Score guidance:

- 5: Full-page screenshot is readable and covers all important sections.
- 3: Major sections are visible, but at least one important section is missing or collapsed.
- 1-2: Only a hero screenshot or unreadable partial screenshots are provided.
- Unknown: Do not score missing sections; mark them `Unknown`.

Avoid:

- Penalizing the profile for sections that were not captured.
- Inferring private settings, hidden contact paths, or search performance from screenshots alone.

### Positioning Clarity

Why it matters:

- AI-assisted search and people-search systems work better with explicit, verifiable criteria. Vague profile language is harder for both humans and AI-assisted workflows to match to a role, buyer, category, or expert query.
- Recent B2B buying research also points to AI-mediated shortlists. A profile that does not make its category and value legible may be skipped before a human evaluates it.

Evaluate:

- Does the headline name role, category, audience, problem, outcome, or proof?
- Can the reader understand the professional promise in the first screen?
- Does the About opening reinforce the same promise?
- Is the positioning specific enough for the chosen goal?

Goal-specific interpretation:

- Job search: target role, seniority, domain, and key skills should be immediately clear.
- Sales: buyer, problem, offer, and outcome should be visible without reading the full profile.
- Founder/investor: market category, company story, and founder credibility should be clear.
- Executive: leadership scope, strategic domain, and authority should be visible.
- Brand: topic lane and audience promise should be obvious.

Score guidance:

- 5: Clear role/category plus audience or proof; no generic filler.
- 3: Understandable professional category, but too broad or under-evidenced.
- 1-2: The profile could fit many unrelated roles or audiences.

### Visual Trust And Hierarchy

Why it matters:

- A profile is a scanning interface. Visual order determines what a recruiter, buyer, investor, or peer notices before deciding whether to continue.
- B2B buying research increasingly treats trust and discovery as linked: being discoverable is not enough if the first impression does not create confidence.

Evaluate:

- Does the banner reinforce category, company, product, market, or professional context?
- Is the photo clear and appropriate for the audience?
- Are headline, role, proof, and contact/follow cues easy to scan?
- Do Featured thumbnails and titles make proof legible before click-through?
- Does visual clutter distract from the intended story?

Score guidance:

- 5: Visual hierarchy quickly supports the stated goal and surfaces proof.
- 3: Professional but generic; no major trust damage, but little strategic value.
- 1-2: Visuals confuse the category, reduce credibility, or bury important signals.

### Credibility Density

Why it matters:

- Recent people-search research favors factual relevance and information utility. Profiles with explicit proof are easier to evaluate than profiles built on broad claims.
- Recent buyer research shows that social proof, third-party validation, and trustworthy information matter before direct contact.

Evaluate:

- Are claims backed by metrics, scope, named products, markets, customers, teams, budgets, publications, awards, talks, certifications, or recognizable employers?
- Does the profile show before/after outcomes rather than only responsibilities?
- Are Featured assets, recommendations, or posts aligned with the claims?
- Are proof points close to the claims they support?

Score guidance:

- 5: Claims are consistently backed by concrete, relevant evidence.
- 3: Some evidence exists, but it is scattered or too generic.
- 1-2: Ambitious claims have little observable proof.

Avoid:

- Inventing metrics. Use placeholders such as `[metric]`, `[customer category]`, or `[proof point]`.
- Treating famous logos as sufficient proof when the role, scope, or outcome is unclear.

### Audience Fit

Why it matters:

- Different goals require different proof. A recruiter wants role fit and skill evidence; a buyer wants problem relevance and risk reduction; an investor wants market and traction signals.
- Recent B2B research highlights independent research, AI discovery, and peer validation. Profiles should speak to the real decision group, not only the most obvious reader.

Evaluate:

- Is the profile written in language the target audience uses?
- Does the proof type match the audience's risk?
- Does the profile answer the reader's likely first question?
- Are secondary readers considered when relevant, such as hidden buyers, hiring managers, executives, investors, or board members?

Score guidance:

- 5: The profile clearly speaks to the selected goal and primary reader.
- 3: The profile is professionally written but audience-neutral.
- 1-2: The profile uses inward-facing biography when reader-facing relevance is needed.

### Search Discoverability

Why it matters:

- LinkedIn Recruiter and Talent Insights rely on structured filters and profile-derived signals such as title, skills, location, industry, function, and years of experience.
- LinkedIn Talent Insights distinguishes explicit skills listed by members from implicit skills extracted from titles, descriptions, and summaries.
- Recent AI-assisted recruiting workflows increase the value of clear, structured profile content because recruiters can instruct systems to find profiles matching specific criteria.

Evaluate:

- Are target role titles and variants present?
- Are domain, industry, tool, method, certification, and technical terms present naturally?
- Are top skills aligned with the profile goal?
- Are location, language, remote, or market signals visible when relevant?
- Do Experience and About reinforce the same searchable terms without keyword stuffing?

Score guidance:

- 5: Search terms are specific, natural, and supported by experience.
- 3: Some relevant keywords exist, but important variants or skill evidence are missing.
- 1-2: The profile is polished prose with weak searchable signal.

Avoid:

- Promising ranking improvements. Score discoverability readiness, not guaranteed search placement.

### Narrative Coherence

Why it matters:

- People and AI-assisted systems both benefit from consistent signals. If headline, About, Featured, Experience, and Activity point to different professional categories, the profile becomes harder to classify and trust.
- Recent people-search research emphasizes information utility and explicit criteria; coherence improves both.

Evaluate:

- Do headline, About, Experience, Featured, Skills, and Activity reinforce the same story?
- Are career transitions explained?
- Does current positioning match current/recent roles?
- Are outdated claims or old proof assets diluting the main story?

Score guidance:

- 5: Sections reinforce one clear professional through-line.
- 3: The story is understandable but fragmented.
- 1-2: The profile reads like unrelated achievements or competing identities.

### Conversion Readiness

Why it matters:

- Recent B2B research shows buyers often research independently before contacting a vendor and use multiple information sources before engaging.
- Gartner's 2026 buyer research shows buyers use AI and digital self-service but still seek validation and decision support. A profile should make the next step low-friction when the goal involves sales, partnerships, hiring, media, or advisory opportunities.

Evaluate:

- Is there a clear next action?
- Is the contact path appropriate to the goal: Connect, Follow, message, website, portfolio, GitHub, company site, calendar, newsletter, lead magnet, or media kit?
- Does Featured reduce friction with proof assets?
- Does About end with a relevant invitation when appropriate?

Score guidance:

- 5: The profile makes a relevant next step obvious and credible.
- 3: Contact is possible but the reason to engage is weak.
- 1-2: Interested readers would not know what to do next.

### Content Authority

Why it matters:

- Recent B2B buying research links discovery, trust, AI-assisted research, and professional content. Activity and Featured content can show judgment before direct contact.
- For recruiters, content authority matters most when public expertise is part of the role. For sales, founder, executive, and brand goals, it can become a primary credibility signal.

Evaluate:

- Are recent posts or comments visible?
- Do posts show judgment, examples, analysis, or original perspective?
- Are themes consistent with the positioning?
- Do Featured items include durable proof, not only ephemeral posts?
- Does content help the audience understand a problem or make a better decision?

Score guidance:

- 5: Activity clearly compounds authority in the intended category.
- 3: Activity exists but the topic lane is inconsistent.
- 1-2: Content is absent, irrelevant, generic, or mostly announcements.
- Unknown: Do not score if Activity is not visible and the user did not provide posts.

## Section Context

### Intro / Hero

Primary role:

- First-screen classification. It should answer: who is this person, what category do they belong to, why are they credible, and what should I do next?

Evaluate:

- Headline specificity.
- Banner relevance.
- Photo trust and appropriateness.
- Current role and company.
- Location, availability, contact, follow/connect cues.
- Goal-specific signal such as Open to Work, creator mode, company affiliation, or services.

### About

Primary role:

- Narrative and proof bridge. It should turn first-screen positioning into a believable professional story.

Evaluate:

- First 2-3 visible lines.
- Reader relevance.
- Proof density.
- Keyword and category language.
- Coherent career story.
- Call to action when useful.

### Featured

Primary role:

- Curated proof shelf. It should make the strongest evidence visible before the reader has to search.

Evaluate:

- Relevance of the first 2-4 assets.
- Clear titles and thumbnails.
- Mix of proof assets: case studies, portfolio, talks, demos, articles, media, GitHub, documents, or customer proof.
- Alignment with the stated goal.

### Activity / Posts

Primary role:

- Current judgment and topical signal. It shows what the person is paying attention to now.

Evaluate:

- Recency.
- Topic consistency.
- Specific examples and expertise.
- Comments in relevant conversations.
- Signal-to-noise ratio.

### Experience

Primary role:

- Evidence backbone. It should prove that the positioning is earned.

Evaluate:

- Role scope.
- Company context.
- Measurable outcomes.
- Progression.
- Keywords and methods.
- Transitions.

### Skills

Primary role:

- Search and evidence alignment. Skills should match the target role, buyer problem, or category and be supported elsewhere.

Evaluate:

- Top skills relevance.
- Coverage of tools, methods, leadership, domain, and technical skills.
- Fit with current target goal.
- Evidence linkage to Experience, Featured, certifications, or recommendations.

### Recommendations / Social Proof

Primary role:

- External validation. It reduces perceived risk when it supports the same claims as the profile.

Evaluate:

- Relevance of recommenders.
- Recency and credibility.
- Specificity of praise.
- Match to the desired positioning.

### Education / Certifications / Projects / Publications

Primary role:

- Optional authority and specialization. These sections should add proof rather than clutter.

Evaluate:

- Credential relevance to the goal.
- Project/publication specificity.
- Recognizable institutions, issuers, or artifacts.
- Whether the section strengthens or distracts from the main positioning.

## How To Use This In Audits

- Do not cite every source in a user-facing audit. Use research to make better scoring decisions.
- Cite sources only when the user asks why a parameter matters or requests a research-backed audit.
- Prefer concise source-backed explanations such as: "I weighted search discoverability higher because LinkedIn recruiter tooling uses profile-derived title, skill, and filter signals, and recent AI-assisted recruiting research increases the value of explicit criteria."
- Keep analysis evidence-led. The screenshot remains the primary source for the individual profile.
